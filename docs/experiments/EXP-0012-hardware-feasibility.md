# EXP-0012 — Hardware feasibility assessment (ANALYTICAL, written after the model results)

No silicon, FPGA, RTL or hardware work was authorized or done. Everything here is ANALYTICAL or INFERRED from `results/predictive_state/cost_table.md`, the EXP-0012 measurements and EXP-0001..0011. Nothing is a hardware result.

## What the model results say the hardware would have to do
Per control step (batch 1), ANALYTICAL, toy-scale network (`cost_table.md`):

| arm | encoder fraction | MACs | weight+state bytes | encoder share of bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G (FP16, full perception) | 1.00 | 1.27 M | 588 kB | 0.53 |
| G-I4a (INT4 all, learned trigger) | 0.33 | 0.53 M | 109 kB | 0.24 |
| G-Tu (ternary updater, FP16 encoder) | 0.33 | 0.53 M | 144 kB | 0.71 |
| G-Ta (ternary all, learned trigger) | 0.33 | 0.53 M | 55 kB | 0.25 |

1. **At this scale there is nothing for a FlexTern processor to solve.** The whole model is 55-590 kB, the recurrent state is 256 B, and a 30 Hz loop needs about 0.5-1.3 M MACs per step (15-40 MMAC/s) and under 20 MB/s of weight traffic. A microcontroller-class NPU already runs that (KNOWN class of hardware; not benchmarked here). The EXP-0006 residency sizing (hundreds of MB) and EXP-0008/0009 SRAM leakage questions concern LLM-scale weights and do not transfer to a 0.1 M-parameter policy. Any commercial relevance would come from real vision backbones and VLA-scale models, which this experiment does not test (SPECULATIVE).
2. **Where the bytes go decides what to specialize.** With a full-precision encoder, ternarizing the updater and heads (G-Tu) cuts weight bytes only 1.7x because the encoder stays at 71% of traffic once it is skipped two thirds of the time; the encoder must also be low-bit (G-Ta) to get the 5-11x byte reduction against FP16. At the toy's scale the encoder is also 85-89% of the MACs, so MAC count falls only through skipping, not through ternary (a ternary MAC is an add: the count is unchanged).
3. **Ternary vs INT4 is at most ~2x on weight bytes, and the measurement agrees.** Same-scope comparison (G-Ta vs G-I4a, same learned trigger): 2.01x bytes, 1.02x MACs (ANALYTICAL). The add-versus-MAC logic advantage is real at the generic-cell level (EXP-0001/0005: ~2.7x fewer cells at 256 terms) but is not an energy or latency measurement at system level, and EXP-0011 showed that realized CPU speed did not follow the weight format (SPEED_LOW). EXP-0012 adds no hardware energy evidence.
4. **Existing NPUs.** Nothing in the measurements requires a new architecture: all arms run as ordinary dense convolutions and GRU matmuls; ternary weights stored at 2 bits and expanded to INT8 on an existing NPU would still get the byte reduction (INFERRED), only without the adder-only logic saving. The open question is whether an NPU can gate its vision encoder cheaply; the gating logic here (a 20 kMAC trigger head or a pixel-difference test) is trivial.
5. **Encoder gating is the largest lever found, and it is not ternary-specific.** About 60% fewer encoder runs kept 91-100% of success in this toy, and a fixed-rate schedule did as well as the learned trigger. It reduces every conventional control equally, so it cannot be claimed as a ternary advantage. MEASURED latency effect on CPU (FP32 reference): 1.4x (learned trigger) to 1.9x (pixel-difference trigger) at ~0.3 encoder fraction.
6. **Persistent state SRAM and idle power.** The 128-d state is trivial to keep on chip; the real question is leakage and wake behaviour of the *weights* between control steps (EXP-0008/0009: unmeasured at modern nodes, temperature-dependent). With a 30 Hz loop and sub-millisecond compute the duty cycle is a few percent, which is the regime where EXP-0009 found residency's advantage most sensitive to retention power. No new evidence either way.
7. **Data movement between prediction and control.** The predictor head is training/analysis-only in this design; deployment executes encoder (gated), updater, policy head and trigger. No inter-stage traffic beyond a 256 B state was found significant.

## Conclusion
EXP-0012 does not provide a hardware case. It shows (a) no precision effect (FP16, INT8, INT4, ternary) was detected among JEPA-trained recurrent policies at this scale, inside a large seed spread, (b) the only >2x efficiency lever measured is skipping perception, which applies to any precision, and (c) ternary vs INT4 is bounded near 2x on bytes and 1x on MACs by arithmetic. What would have to be true for a FlexTern hardware thesis to survive this line of work: the encoder (not the updater) is ternarized at real scale without quality loss (BitVLA reports this for a feed-forward VLA; not reproduced here), gating policies keep real-robot success, and an existing NPU cannot achieve comparable energy with INT4/INT8. None of those is tested here.
