## Research Question

Across BitNet b1.58 2B4T, Falcon-E 1B and Falcon-E 3B, under combinations of context, batch, external bandwidth, ternary throughput, packing, activation precision, head and KV precision, and on-chip residency: which component limits autoregressive decode, and how large can the end-to-end benefit of ternary specialization be before the non-ternary parts cap it?

## Hypothesis

The founder's product hypothesis under test: shorter-context, memory-constrained decode offers the most ternary-only headroom; long-context serving may require non-ternary optimization. Not assumed; evaluated.

## Why It Matters

Multi-fold end-to-end gains are central to the thesis. Amdahl's law says the non-improved fraction caps everything. Before any datapath or memory-feed hardware, FlexTern needs to know where that cap sits per workload region (ADR-0005 scope).

## Setup

Inputs: the three canonical `model_summary.json` and `weight_distribution_by_module.csv` files (ADR-0004), loaded programmatically; identity and revision asserted at startup; KV and terms-per-byte invariants asserted against EXP-0002B.

Five components per generated token at batch B and context C (equations in `analysis/roofline_models.py`):

| Component | Compute (ops/token) | External bytes/token |
|---|---|---|
| A. Ternary linears | W terms (dense); W(1−z) under ideal zero-skip | W·bits/8 ÷ B (stream once per step, shared by B sequences) |
| B. Output head | V·H MACs | V·H·b_head ÷ B |
| C. Attention | L·(2·nH·hd·C + 4·nH·C) op-eq (QKᵀ, AV, softmax ≈ 4 ops/score) | — |
| D. KV cache | — | read 2·nKV·hd·b_kv·L·C + write 2·nKV·hd·b_kv·L (sequence-specific, not shared) |
| E. Other | norms 3/elem, activation quant 2/elem over 4 inputs/layer, scale 1/output, RoPE, residual, activation fn, gating | 2·(norms + scales) ÷ B |

Activations are assumed on-chip and reported as counts only. Compute per token does not shrink with batch; weight bytes do; KV bytes do not.

Time per stage: compute_i = ops/rate, memory_i = bytes/BW. **Optimistic** T = Σ max(compute_i, memory_i); **conservative** T = Σ (compute_i + memory_i). No cross-stage prefetch in either.

**Ternary-only Amdahl ceilings** (optimistic bound): compute-only elimination sets the ternary stage to its memory time; bandwidth-only sets it to its compute time; ideal sets it to zero. Ceiling = T/T′.

**Memory-bound identity.** When every stage is memory-bound, T = bytes/BW and the ideal ceiling = total bytes / non-ternary bytes, independent of compute rates and overlap. Hence, at batch 1 with KV1 = 2·nKV·hd·b_kv·L:

- ideal ceiling = 2× when KV·C = W·bits/8 − head − norms, i.e. C₂ = (W·bits/8 − V·H·b_head − norms)/KV1
- ideal ceiling = 3× when KV·C = W·bits/16 − head − norms
- KV > ternary: C = (W·bits/8)/KV1; KV > head: C = V·H·b_head/KV1; KV > all weights: C = (W·bits/8 + V·H·b_head + norms)/KV1

**Ternary-stage break-even** (dense, terms/byte = 8/bits = 4): compute = memory when rate = BW·4·B; bandwidth to feed an engine = rate/(4B); lanes to saturate BW at clock f = BW·4·B/f; batch to balance = rate/(4·BW). Model-independent.

Abstract defaults (assumptions, not targets): 250 GB/s; 4096 terms/cycle at 1 GHz (4.1e12 terms/s); 1e12 non-ternary op-eq/s; BF16 head and KV; 2-bit weights; dense; batch 1; context 4096. Sweeps: context 128–32768 (flagged hypothetical above a model's maximum); batch 1–32; 100 GB/s–8 TB/s; 128–131072 terms/cycle; 250 MHz–2 GHz; 0.25–8e12 non-ternary; SRAM 0–1 GB with seven residency policies; tiles 128/256/512.

### Batch

Weights amortize ÷B; KV does not; per-token compute is unchanged. Batch therefore lowers the ternary-only ceiling at fixed context (Falcon 1B at 4k: 2.69× → 1.74× → 1.35× for B = 1/4/16 at 100 GB/s). Batch at which streamed weight bytes equal KV read bytes: Falcon 1B 42 (512 ctx), 5.3 (4k), 0.7 (32k); BitNet 30 / 3.7; Falcon 3B 52 / 6.4 / 0.8. Batch to balance the ternary stage's own compute against its weight stream: B* = rate/(4·BW), e.g. 10.2 at 100 GB/s and 1.0 at 1 TB/s for 4096 lanes at 1 GHz.

### Bandwidth and compute break-even (model-independent for dense 2-bit)

| BW | terms/s to saturate (B=1) | lanes @1 GHz | lanes @250 MHz |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 GB/s | 4e11 | 400 | 1,600 |
| 250 GB/s | 1e12 | 1,000 | 4,000 |
| 1 TB/s | 4e12 | 4,000 | 16,000 |

At the edge-like operating points (100–250 GB/s, batch 1), a few hundred to a thousand Y-Core lanes at 1 GHz saturate the weight stream. Ternary compute is the limiter only when the engine is narrower than that, when bandwidth exceeds ~1 TB/s with a 4096-lane engine, when batch ≥ ~10 at 100 GB/s, or during prefill.

A one-layer working buffer removes nothing from stream-once decode. For Falcon-E, 256 MB holding the head leaves only KV as non-ternary traffic, so at short context the ternary-only ceiling becomes very large; for BitNet the head needs ~1 GB. No capacity below 1 GB changes the long-context KV conclusion. None of these capacities is claimed to be economically realistic.

### Packing (5 trits/byte, hypothetical, decoder cost unmeasured)

Ternary bytes fall 20% (521 → 417 MB BitNet; 396 → 317 MB Falcon 1B). Ternary stage time falls 20% in the memory-bound regime; the ternary-only *ceiling* falls (Falcon 1B S0: 3.70× → 3.16×) because less of the system is ternary afterwards. The 2× boundary context moves earlier (Falcon 1B 10,658 → 7,433). Packing is a realization of ternary opportunity, not an expansion of it.

### Activation precision (8-bit measured; 4-bit hypothetical)

At every bandwidth-bound point, 4-bit activations change nothing in the token model (activations are on-chip). Accumulator width falls (K = 2048: 20 → 16 bits format; 13312: 22 → 18). A hypothetical bit-plane engine twice as fast changes nothing while the ternary stage is memory-limited. Q-021 stays open.

### KV precision (BF16 reference; INT8/INT4 hypothetical, system-ceiling exploration only)

In the memory-bound regime, INT4 KV raises Falcon 1B's 32k byte ratio from 1.42 to 2.18 and moves its 2× boundary from 10.7k context to beyond the model's supported 32k context; INT8 KV to 13.4k. In the modeled S6 scenario the token time does not improve, because attention compute at the assumed 1e12 op-eq/s (3.2 ms at 32k for Falcon 1B) already exceeds the KV read time at 250 GB/s. KV compression only pays if attention compute keeps pace (Q-022). No claim is made that these models tolerate reduced KV precision.

### Head precision (BF16 measured; INT8/INT4 hypothetical, no quality claim)

Hypothetical INT8 head lifts BitNet S0 from 1.75× to 2.41× and S1 from 1.54× to 1.81×; INT4 to 3.55× / 2.09×. For Falcon-E the head is already small; INT8 lifts S0 from 3.70× to 5.96×.

### Zero handling

Dense, zero-gated, and ideal-skip share identical packed bytes. Ideal skipping (41% fewer terms) changes nothing at any bandwidth-bound point in the sweeps; it would matter only where the ternary stage is compute-limited.

### Tiles and accumulators

All five observed K divide by 128 and 256; only 6912 fails 512 (14 tiles, 96.4% tail utilization). Accumulators: 20/20/21/22/22 bits (format) for K = 2048/2560/6912/9216/13312 at 8-bit activations; 16/16/17/18/18 at 4-bit. No tile is selected.

### Prefill (concise)

Weights stream once per prompt, so the ternary stage is compute-bound at any realistic bandwidth (4.1 MB/token of weights at P = 128). Causal attention compute overtakes ternary compute only at P* = W/(nH·hd·L) = 27k / 32k / 45k tokens, so prefill is the most ternary-compute-dominated regime (61–98% of modeled time for P ≤ 4096). Prefill is where ternary compute throughput, not bandwidth, is the lever.

## Interpretation

### The sixteen key questions

1. **Batch-1 dominance.** BitNet: non-ternary (head) dominates bytes at every context. Falcon-E 1B/3B: ternary dominates up to ~10.7k/18.2k context (KV + head crossover), then KV.
2. **KV overtakes ternary weights** at 6.8k (BitNet, above its 4k max), 16.1k (1B), 22.3k (3B).
3. **KV + head exceed ternary** always (BitNet), 10.7k (1B), 18.2k (3B).
4. **Ideal ceiling by context:** table above. Falcon-E: ≥3× below ~2.6k/7k, ≥2× below ~10.7k/18k, 1.42×/1.60× at 32k. BitNet: ≤1.78× everywhere.
5. **2× possible from ternary alone:** Falcon-E batch 1 up to ~10.7k (1B) / ~18k (3B) context in the bandwidth-bound regime; never for BitNet with a BF16 head.
6. **3× possible:** Falcon-E batch 1 up to ~2.6k (1B) / ~7k (3B) context at 100 GB/s; at 1 TB/s only ≤512 / ≤1024. These are ceilings on *further* ternary optimization. The advantage *already realized* by 2-bit storage against an INT4-weight system with identical head and KV is 1.3–1.8× in bytes; against BF16-weight systems 3–7×.
7. **2× impossible without non-ternary work:** BitNet at any context (head); every model beyond ~11–18k context (KV); every model at batch ≥ 4 beyond 2k–4k context; every model at ≥1 TB/s beyond 2k–4k context (attention compute at the assumed rate).
8. **Batching** shrinks the weight streams but not KV, lowering the ternary-only ceiling at fixed context (Falcon 1B 4k: 2.69× → 1.35× at B = 16). Batch raises throughput but shifts the limiter to KV and attention.
9. **5-trit packing** cuts ternary time 20% but lowers the remaining ceiling and pulls the 2× boundary earlier (Falcon 1B 10.7k → 7.4k). It helps absolute performance, not the share of opportunity, and its decoder cost is unmeasured.
10. **Lower-precision KV** would move Falcon 1B's 2× boundary from 10.7k to 13.4k (INT8) context, or beyond the model's supported 32k (INT4), in the memory-bound regime, but only if attention compute keeps pace; hypothetical and quality-unproven.
11. **SRAM:** 256 MB holding Falcon-E's head leaves KV as the only non-ternary stream (short-context ceiling > 20×); BitNet's head needs ~1 GB; no capacity ≤ 1 GB changes the 32k-context KV conclusion.
12. **Short-context edge wedge:** supported for modest-vocabulary models (Falcon-E class) at batch 1: ceilings of 3.7–5.8× at 512 context versus 1.4–1.6× at 32k. Not supported for 128k-vocabulary models with a BF16 head (BitNet ceiling 1.75× at 512 context). The conclusion is vocabulary-dependent.
13. **Long-context serving** requires KV (and attention compute) optimization to retain multi-fold gains: ceilings fall to 1.16–1.60× at 32k.
14. **Accumulator/compute optimization** is not the lever at bandwidth-bound edge points (400–1,000 lanes at 1 GHz saturate 100–250 GB/s); it becomes the lever for prefill, batch ≥ ~10 at 100 GB/s, bandwidth ≥ ~1 TB/s with a 4k-lane engine, or engines narrower than the break-even lane count.
15. **Thesis:** reshaped rather than strengthened or weakened. The ternary datapath is necessary but carries multi-fold system gains only in short-context, batch-1, modest-vocabulary decode; everywhere else the system-level scope of ADR-0005 (head, KV, attention) is where the gain is decided.
16. **Most valuable uncertainty to reduce next:** whether a bandwidth-matched streaming ternary engine (unpack + Y-Core lanes + 20–22-bit accumulation at K = 2048–13312) can actually be built to consume packed weights at line rate at the edge operating point. Everything above assumes it can; nothing physical has been measured.

### Where ternary-only optimization is insufficient

| Region | Cap | Gating component |
|---|---|---|
| 128k-vocabulary models with BF16 head, any context | ≤1.8× | output head |
| Any model, context ≥ ~11–18k, batch 1 | ≤2× | KV cache (bytes) |
| Any model, batch ≥ 4, context ≥ 2–4k | ≤2× | KV cache (bytes) |
| Any model, BW ≥ ~1 TB/s, context ≥ 2–4k | ≤2× | attention compute at the assumed rate (Q-022) |
| Prefill | ternary compute dominates; the cap is the engine's terms/s, not bandwidth | ternary compute |

### Assumptions strengthened / weakened

Strengthened: the system-level scope (ADR-0005); the "ternary-first, not ternary-only" framing; the edge/short-context wedge for Falcon-class models. Weakened: any expectation of multi-fold gains at long context or high batch from the datapath alone; the Phase 2 handoff's implied priority on accumulator RTL for decode; the relevance of zero skipping at bandwidth-bound points.

## Surprises

- The ternary compute needed to saturate an edge-class memory bus is small: 400 lanes at 1 GHz for 100 GB/s. The datapath question becomes "stream at line rate," not "compute faster."
- Residency of a 134 MB head turns Falcon-E's short-context ceiling from ~3.7× to >20×; the head, not the ternary stream, is the first thing worth keeping on-chip for modest-vocabulary models.
- Hypothetical INT4 KV did not change the modeled 32k token time at all, because attention compute at the abstract rate was already larger than the KV read time.
- Batching reduces the ternary-only ceiling, since the only stream it does not amortize is the sequence-specific KV cache.

## Limitations

- Abstract rates everywhere; the non-ternary compute rate in particular has no evidence (Q-022).
- Stream-once, no cross-stage prefetch, no activation traffic, softmax at 4 ops/score, "other" at coarse op counts.
- Three checkpoints from two families; one quantization recipe.
- Context sweeps above a model's maximum are hypothetical (BitNet above 4096).
- No quality claim for any hypothetical head, KV, or activation precision.
- No plots; tables only.

## What This Proves

- Under the stated accounting, the component byte and op counts per token for the three workloads and the Amdahl ceilings they imply.
- The analytical crossover and break-even equations, confirmed by sweep transitions.

## What This Does NOT Prove

- Any achievable speedup. Ceilings are upper bounds on an impossible ideal.
- Any hardware capability, bandwidth, clock, area, power, or tokens/sec.
- That any component is cheap to build.
