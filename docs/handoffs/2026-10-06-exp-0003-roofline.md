## Important Context

- Memory-bound identity: ideal ceiling = total bytes / non-ternary bytes. Use it to sanity-check any new scenario before trusting the tables.
- Ceilings rise at high bandwidth with narrow engines because the ternary stage becomes compute-limited; that is slow ternary compute, not a favorable byte ratio. Read `ternary_limiter` alongside the ceiling.
- 5-trit packing and ternary residency *lower* the ternary-only ceiling because they realize ternary opportunity; head/KV residency *raise* it.
- Residency policies invent no hit rates; "ideal" policies are upper bounds.

## Recommended Immediate Next Consideration

EXP-0004: a bandwidth-matched streaming ternary dot-product engine study — RTL plus generic synthesis of a 256–1,024-lane engine that consumes packed 2-bit bytes (four output rows per byte) at line rate, with 20–22-bit accumulation over K = 2048–13312 in 256-wide tiles, measuring cells and logic depth against the lanes-per-bandwidth break-even (400 lanes at 1 GHz per 100 GB/s). Every EXP-0003 ceiling assumes this engine exists and is cheap; nothing physical has been measured. This is memory-feed plus accumulation sized by evidence, not the accumulator-topology study proposed after EXP-0002, and it is chosen over KV-compression research because the ternary engine's feasibility is the premise of all other work while the KV question depends on a market decision (Q-019). Caveat: generic synthesis yields cells and logic depth, not clock, so EXP-0004 can establish whether a line-rate engine is structurally plausible; "line rate at 1 GHz" would remain INFERRED until a physical target exists.
